**Abstract:**
The Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) connects medical devices to clinical systems but also creates new security risks. Cyberattacks on these systems can disrupt patient care and expose sensitive information. Although machine learning, federated learning, and medical IoT intrusion detection often report high benchmark accuracy, their performance beyond shared benchmark datasets remains unclear. Therefore, this study conducted a PRISMA 2020 systematic review of primary studies published in 2025 and 2026. Fourteen predefined searches identified 690 records, with 594 remaining after duplicate removal. Following title, venue, and abstract screening, 112 studies underwent quality assessment. Of these, 83 scored at least 75 out of 100 on a ten-item instrument, and 50 were selected using a predefined thematic and geographic allocation rule. Each study was coded using 29 variables linked to supporting evidence, with the full text of 49 of the 50 studies read to confirm the benchmark, deployment, and metric reporting variables. Non-parametric statistical tests were then applied. Only 27 studies reported comparable detection accuracy, with a median of 99.03\% (95\% CI: 98.00-99.47). Among these studies, 51.9\% reported at least 99\% accuracy. However, no association was detected between accuracy and methodological rigor ($\rho=+0.024$, $p=0.905$, $n=27$; all Holm-adjusted $p=1.000$). Moreover, 82\% reported baseline comparisons, 44\% reported a metric beyond accuracy, and 22\% reported dispersion in their main results. In addition, 37 studies relied only on offline public datasets, six validated on named constrained hardware, and one used real multi-site clinical data. Accuracy did not differ across benchmark groups ($p=0.896$). Overall, high reported accuracy alone does not establish clinical deployment readiness. Stronger reporting practices are needed to evaluate the reliability of these methods.
